The philosophy

We don't develop
the mind. We unfold it.

IFSU stands for International Foundation for Spiritual Unfoldment. That word — unfoldment, or opening out — is at the heart of everything. The mind is already complete. It doesn't need improving. It needs the veils that cover it removed.

According to Gururaj Ananda Yogi, what there is to find already exists. It is enough to remove what covers it.

“It is the difference between sailing a boat on the surface of the ocean — battered by the waves — and diving into the depths. In the depths, you are no longer tormented by the waves, even though you are still aware of them.”

— Gururaj Ananda Yogi, Path of Unfoldment

Meditation does not suppress the impressions stored in the subconscious — the samskaras, as Gururaj calls them. It transcends them. It loosens their grip. And gradually, the veils lift.

The origin

Gururaj
Ananda Yogi

Born in 1932 in Gujarat (India). At 5, he left the family home in search of a direct experience of the Divine. At 16, under the guidance of Swami Pavitrananda in the Himalayas, he attained what the traditions call Self-realisation.

He didn't stay in a Himalayan cave. Pavitrananda asked him to immerse himself in the Western world — to marry, raise a family, succeed in business — to better understand Westerners' lives before guiding them. Which he did.

“What I understand now is that true realisation is nothing like what one imagines. My mind, my body, my personality — they were only the reflection of my true Nature. I was the same energy as everything around me.”

Gururaj Ananda Yogi

Objections & questions

Real questions
deserve real answers.

What's the difference from breath- or thought-observation methods? +
According to the teaching of Gururaj Ananda Yogi, IFSU Vedic meditation aims to transcend the mind, not just observe it. It works at the subconscious and superconscious levels, where, according to the tradition, tensions have their roots.
Is it a cult? +
No. IFSU is non-sectarian, non-dogmatic, compatible with all beliefs — or none. Gururaj himself insisted that IFSU should not become a cult of the Guru. No doctrine to adopt. Our motto: if you're a Christian, you'll become a better Christian. If you're an atheist, you'll become a better person. These are techniques — like learning to swim. You don't need to believe. You practise and you observe.
How much time does it take each day? +
You start small: 20 minutes, once a day. The practice builds up session after session. That isn't arbitrary: it's this progression that lets the techniques work in depth, at the subconscious level. If even the first 20 minutes seem out of reach right now, the Meditation Discovery workshop is a lighter entry point, with no daily commitment, to lay the foundations before considering the full cycle.
Why €250 for the full cycle? +
€250 is 10 individual sessions of 2 to 3 hours, i.e. €25 a session, follow-up included. You don't pay for the techniques. You pay for the time of transmission and follow-up. Payment in two instalments at most. After the cycle, you can join the Monday group at no extra cost.
Does it really work? +
I promise no result. I give you a precise method and I tell you what practitioners report. You judge from your own practice, not from my word. That's why we begin with a no-commitment meeting — and if you want to try without committing to the cycle, the Discovery workshop is made for that.
I don't believe in spirituality. +
No problem. You don't need to believe in it. What we teach: breathing techniques (pranayama), concentration techniques (tratak, mantra), exercises for coming back when thoughts spin. You can practise all of it without signing up to anything spiritual. If a spiritual dimension opens up over time, that's your choice — not an obligation.
Can I learn this on my own, from a book or a video? +
You can read a lot of things. Techniques prescribed for you can't be read. What matters is that it's given to you at the right moment, to your measure, and that it's corrected if it goes astray. A book can't see what you're doing. I can.
What if I can't do it, if I've never meditated? +
That's the most common case. You have nothing to get right: you have techniques to practise. When something sticks, we look together at where, and adjust. That's precisely why the cycle is individual. And if, despite that, individual meditation really blocks you, the Discovery workshop is a lighter way in: 21 days, three workshops in a small group of five and 15 guided audios, with an “Anti-Blocks” guide. You lay the foundations at your own pace, then we talk about the cycle again.
What does a session look like? +
We meet in Ath, face to face (or by video call, if you're far away). You practise with me, I pass things on, we talk about what happened. Between sessions, you practise at home. I stay reachable if something sticks.
Do you hold my hand during the cycle? +
No. I give you the techniques, I help if you get stuck, I advise you. You do the work. That's what makes the practice solid: it belongs to you.
What happens if I stop partway? +
Nothing dramatic. You keep what you've received. If you resume later, we start from where you are, not from zero. But I'll tell you frankly if I sense that stopping is giving up rather than a choice.
Can I pay in instalments? +
Yes, in one or two payments at most, on dates we agree together. Write to me, we'll see what suits you.
Are there any contraindications? +
This practice is not a treatment. If you're going through a heavy psychological period, talk to your doctor or a health professional before starting, and tell me at our first conversation. I'd rather we postponed than you put yourself in difficulty. If you're already seeing a therapist, the two approaches are compatible — letting your therapist know is a good idea, they can tell you whether the time is right.
What if something unpleasant comes up during the practice? +
It happens. It can be fear, anger or inner tension, during or after meditation. The IFSU tradition describes this as a release of tension. We don't resist it, we don't judge ourselves, we come out of the meditation slowly. Tell me: if you're struggling a lot, we shorten the sessions to ten minutes. It's an adjustment, not a failure. If it goes beyond what I can support, I'll point you to a professional.
Who can start? +
Any adult who is curious and ready to practise every day. No need for physical fitness, equipment or any particular level.
Is it compatible with my religion? +
Yes. The IFSU emblem represents 9 great wisdom traditions — Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Judaism, Hinduism, Taoism, Zoroastrianism, Confucianism, and Philosophy. The teaching contradicts none of these traditions. It can only deepen them.
